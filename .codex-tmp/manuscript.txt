CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
Cebu's social life is evolving as people seek not only prominent attractions but also secret or lesser-known locations (often called "spots") where friends can rest, interact, and unwind [1]. Numerous clubs, bars, cafés, stores, restaurants, and hangout areas exist in cities such as Cebu, yet many of these locations, particularly lesser-known ones, go unnoticed by the general public. More and more tourists are exploring local sights and looking for quick, entertaining, and pleasurable vacation options close to home.
Most people use social media and influencers to find venues to hang out [2, 36, 37, 43]. These sources are somewhat useful, although they often lack structure, reliability, and up-to-date information. Additionally, some of these sources won't live up to users' expectations once they arrive in the area, where they're not what they seem. Online content is sometimes curated and scripted by the influencers and earners [44].
On the other hand, making reservations in high-end clubs, bars, and restaurants is often inconvenient due to manual booking processes and limited digital platforms [3, 39, 40, 45].
To address these challenges, this concept paper proposes developing CebSpot, an application that showcases both popular and hidden or low-key hangout spots. The application will allow users to discover places, rate and recommend venues, and reserve tables or time slots at selected high-end locations, such as clubs, bars, and restaurants.
1.2 Statement of the Problem
Despite the availability of many social and hangout locations, the following problems are observed:
1. How can users effectively discover hidden or low-key hangout spots that are not widely promoted on mainstream platforms?
2. How can information about social venues be consolidated into a single, reliable, and centralized platform?
3. How can the reservation process in high-end clubs, bars, and restaurants be made more efficient and less time-consuming through digital means?
4. How can a platform provide users with a trusted, centralized space to rate, review, and recommend hangout spots while addressing the issue of scripted or fake content?
1.3 Objectives of the Study
The general objective of this study is to develop CebSpot. This mobile application enables users to discover popular and hidden spots, rate and recommend venues, and reserve tables in selected high-end locations.
Specific Objectives
To design a user-friendly mobile application for spot discovery.
To implement a table reservation system for high-end clubs and venues.
To integrate a map feature that displays nearby, trending, and suggested spots.
To provide venue owners with a platform to promote their establishments.
To integrate a secure payment system that allows users to process reservations and transactions within the application.
1.4 Significance of the Study
The proposed system will benefit the following:
Users / Customers: The application will make it easier to locate, reserve tables, and arrange events.
Owners: Businesses may boost their visibility, manage reservations more easily, and attract more customers.
Tourists: Visitors can easily find alternatives in unfamiliar regions.
Tourism agencies: The system can be used as a supplement to promote local and social venues, allowing agencies to showcase locations better, support tourism campaigns, and give travelers accurate, up-to-date information about local hangouts.
Students and Researchers: The work may be used as a reference for future research on mobile reservations and location-based systems.
1.5 Scope and Limitations
1.5.1 Scope
This research focuses on the development of CebSpot, a smartphone application designed to address the fragmented nature of venue discovery and leisure planning in Cebu City. The scope includes the research, design, development, and evaluation of a digital platform that integrates location-based services, peer-verified ratings, and booking capabilities to improve the way customers discover and access attractions in Cebu. The application is designed for the Android platform, ensuring that it is accessible to a wide range of mobile device users.
Geographic Coverage:
The study is limited to venues in Cebu City and its immediate vicinity, including key social districts, entertainment areas, and emerging recreational spots that the target group typically visits. This also covers the better-known giants and some lesser-known or hidden flags that are sometimes ignored in media coverage.
Target Establishments:
CebSpot will include nightclubs, bars, cafés, restaurants, stalls, and other social gathering spots. The platform prioritizes establishments that provide leisure and entertainment over critical services, emphasizing areas where individuals can socialize, rest, and unwind.
Core Functionalities:
With the application's GPS-based venue-finding feature, users can locate nearby venues from their current position. The review system includes trust verification tools to address the acknowledged issue of scripted and fraudulent content from influencers [36, 44]. Users can search, filter by preferences such as category, price, distance, and rating, and browse detailed venue information. The booking feature will allow digital reservations at participating locations [39, 40, 45].
Target User Groups:
The study focuses on Cebu locals who actively pursue leisure and social activities, tourists and visitors interested in what the city offers, and local venue owners seeking a digital presence. The platform caters to both Cebu locals looking to explore new spots and outsiders seeking solid recommendations.
Research Components:
The descriptive aspect will involve surveys to understand user needs and preferences, interviews with venue owners to identify operational requirements, and analysis of current venue discovery methods in Cebu. The developmental aspect covers system design, feature implementation, usability testing, and performance evaluation. Data collection will examine user behavior patterns, platform effectiveness, and satisfaction metrics.
Technical Implementation:
The study includes mobile application development for Android, database design for venue information and user data, implementation of GPS and mapping technologies, development of the recommendation algorithm, creation of the trust verification system for reviews, and integration with external APIs for navigation and payment processing, where applicable.
Community Engagement:
The platform will feature community upvoting and public forums where citizens can post and share additional context or notes regarding their experiences.
1.5.2 Limitations
This study's scope limitations include data accuracy issues, selective venue coverage, limited personalized recommendations, language constraints, internet accessibility requirements, challenges in maintaining real-time updates, reliance on user participation for community engagement, reliance on venue operator cooperation for booking features, and limitations in map accuracy and coverage.
Data Accuracy: The platform's venue data will be sourced from available sources, such as business listings, user submissions, and public information, which may not always be up to date or entirely accurate. Operating hours, contact information, and menu options may change without an immediate update to the platform.
Spot Selection: The study will focus on chosen venues based on user recommendations, popularity data, and accessibility, rather than covering every establishment in Cebu City. Some lesser-known or recently opened venues may not be included in the first database.
User-Specific Recommendations: While the software will make recommendations based on location, category, and rating, highly tailored suggestions that account for individual dietary restrictions, unique accessibility requirements, or personal taste preferences may be limited. The system will provide recommendations based on general popularity and user input patterns.
Language Accessibility: The platform's initial version will primarily focus on English and Filipino languages, which may limit accessibility for non-English/non-Filipino speakers or overseas travelers who speak other languages.
Internet Availability: The platform's effectiveness will be defined by its internet connectivity. Most capabilities, including GPS tracking, map loading, real-time updates, and booking services, require an active internet connection. Users in places with inadequate internet connectivity may have limited functionality.
Real-Time Information Updates: Since the study relies on manual updates and user reporting, it may not provide real-time information on venue availability, current crowd numbers, or sudden closures.
Booking Functionality Limitations: The booking feature's effectiveness depends on venue operators' participation and willingness to integrate with the platform. Not all venues may handle digital reservations, and booking confirmations may require manual verification from institution owners rather than immediate automatic confirmation.
Community Engagement: The success of the review system, social features, and community- building aspects will depend heavily on active user participation, which is beyond direct control. The quality and quantity of user-generated content will influence the overall value of the application.
Payment Processing Scope: The platform will handle reservation deposit payments where applicable, but full payment processing for meals, services, or complete transactions is outside the scope. Users must settle full payments directly with the venue upon arrival.
Geographic Coverage Constraints: During the development and testing stages, the platform will concentrate on the Cebu metropolitan area. Expansion into other Philippine cities or regions is anticipated in future phases but is not included in the scope of this initial study.
1.6 Definition of Terms
Agile development is an iterative software development technique that emphasizes continuous improvement, frequent testing, and incremental feature delivery.
Discovery (Tourism Context): The process of selecting and exploring new, emerging, or lesser-known areas for leisure and recreation purposes.
Hangout Spots: Cafés, bars, restaurants, and recreational areas are designated gathering places for people and groups to socialize.
High-End Establishments: Premium venues are distinguished by their luxury services, ambiance, and offerings, which often require advance bookings.
Hidden or Lowkey Spots: Locations that are not well-known or frequently promoted but offer unique or valuable experiences to tourists.
Mobile Application: A software application designed to run on mobile devices like smartphones and tablets, allowing users to access services and perform certain tasks.
Reliability of Information: Refers to the degree to which information is accurate, consistent, and dependable for user decision-making.
Reservation System: A digital tool that allows people to arrange and secure reservations for services or spaces in businesses like restaurants, pubs, and clubs.
Social Media Influence: The impact of preferences and decision-making habits. Content distributed via social media platforms affects people's perceptions.
Spot: A modern term denoting a distinct site or venue noted for its social, recreational, or leisure value, which can range from well-known establishments to less-known spots that provide unique experiences for individuals or groups.
User-Generated Content: Digital content, such as ratings, reviews, and recommendations, created and shared by users on a platform.
CHAPTER 2
REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
This chapter presents a collection of literature and studies that are relevant to the major concepts explored in CebSpot: A Mobile Application for Discovery and Leisure in Cebu City. The materials gathered and analyzed from existing theses, public articles, and scholarly works aim to provide a deeper understanding of the study's foundational elements. The review is organized into six thematic areas: Location-Based Services and Mobile Applications; Social and Location-Based Networking; Point-of-Interest Recommendation Systems; Online Reviews and User Trust; Location Privacy and Data Security; and Mobile Application Adoption in the Philippine Context.
2.1 Related Literatures
2.1.1 Location-Based Services and Mobile Applications
Location-Based Services (LBS) have become a cornerstone of current mobile application development, allowing users to perform a variety of functions based on their geographic location. Akinleye et al. [1] demonstrated the importance of location-based services (LBS) applications in smartphones by combining GPS technology with web services to provide users with real-time spatial information. Their study underscored the practical utility of LBS for everyday navigation and discovery tasks, serving as the technical backbone of applications such as CebSpot.
Building on this, Yin, Ding, and Wang [2] suggested a mobile marketing recommendation method that uses user location data to deliver individualized recommendations. Their findings are pertinent to CebSpot's recommendation engine, demonstrating how location data can improve the relevance and timeliness of venue suggestions for users seeking hangout spots in Cebu City.
The integration of GPS and digital mapping into mobile platforms has also greatly reduced the barrier to consumers exploring unknown locations. By embedding an interactive map within CebSpot, users can visibly discover nearby venues, eliminating the reliance on word-of-mouth or social media influencers—which, while popular, are sometimes unreliable and inconsistently updated.
2.1.2 Location-Based Social Networks
Location-Based Social Networks (LBSNs) are an evolution of traditional social networking, in which geographic context is incorporated into social interactions and content exchange. Cheng, Yang, and Xia [3] presented iZone, a mobile social networking system that links users based on physical proximity and common destinations. Their work demonstrates how spatial data can enhance social experiences, enabling communities to form around shared locations—a premise that underpins CebSpot's community review and rating capabilities.
Zhang et al. [4] expanded on this concept with Hoodsquare, a system that models and suggests communities based on data acquired from location-based social networks. Their findings showed that collective check-in behavior and spatial patterns might be used to describe local locations and yield neighborhood-level insights. For CebSpot, this means aggregated user activity data from Cebu City hangout spots can be used to identify trending locales and emerging social hubs.
By bridging the gap between digital and physical places, LBSNs enable the coordination of daily social life, according to Saker's [7] analysis of locative media and sociability. According to his research, platforms that combine social features with location awareness promote more frequent and significant offline encounters. These results are consistent with CebSpot's design philosophy, which seeks to serve as a catalyst for genuine social interactions among Cebu users and as a discovery tool.
A location recommendation paradigm for LBSNs was proposed by Ye et al. [8], combining social correlation and spatial influence to produce relevant venue recommendations. Their method verifies CebSpot's multi-layered recommendation mechanism, which accounts for community involvement, user preferences, and proximity. Similarly, Chorley et al. [9] investigated the relationship between user personality and LBSN engagement and concluded that personal characteristics influence how users engage with location-based features—a factor pertinent to CebSpot's user profile and preference system.
2.1.3 Point-of-Interest Recommendation Systems
Point-of-Interest (POI) recommendation is an important research topic within the broader field of location-aware computing. Kim, Kim, and Chung [5] created a successor POI recommendation system that incorporates local differential privacy to protect user data while still providing accurate location suggestions. Their work is essential for CebSpot because it demonstrates that effective recommendation methods do not have to compromise user privacy—a balance the program must strike as it manages sensitive location and behavioral data.
Lim et al. [6] proposed an origin-aware next-destination recommendation system with individualized preference attention to predict where users will travel next based on their starting location and historical preferences. This study has clear implications for CebSpot's recommendation module, notably by proposing contextually relevant venues based on the user's current location and previous behavior in Cebu City.
These tests confirm the technical viability of intelligent POI recommendation in mobile applications, supporting the architectural rationale for CebSpot's venue-finding and suggestion functionalities. By incorporating collaborative filtering and contextual cues, CebSpot can go beyond generic listings to provide recommendations that are truly useful and particularly relevant to each user.
2.1.4 Online Reviews, User Trust, and Recommendation Platforms
The role of user-generated reviews in shaping consumer decisions has been extensively studied in the context of digital platforms. Martínez-Navalón, Gelashvili, and Gómez-Ortega [10] evaluated user satisfaction and trust in review platforms, with a focus on TripAdvisor. Their research found that privacy perceptions and electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) significantly influence trust and continued usage of review platforms. For CebSpot, this underscores the importance of building a transparent and credible review ecosystem where users feel confident that the ratings they see reflect genuine experiences. Research has further confirmed that user-generated content, including reviews, ratings, and comments, serves as external stimuli that shape consumer impressions and build online trust [41]. Studies have also shown that users' trust in recommender systems and mobile applications is significantly influenced by the perceived reliability and relevance of the content presented to them [42].
Singh and Kumar [11] conducted a thorough evaluation of the reliability of online reviews. They identified significant criteria such as reviewer competence, review depth, and platform design as predictors of perceived trustworthiness. Their findings indicate that CebSpot's rating and review system should include design elements that enhance credibility, such as verified user identities, thorough review prompts, and systems to detect deceptive content. This is especially important given that the original motive for launching CebSpot was to address inaccuracies in social media- and influencer-driven venue recommendations.
CebSpot can provide users with a more reliable alternative to carefully curated influencer content by incorporating an organized, community-driven review mechanism. The platform's venues will be assessed based on the real-world experiences of guests, giving potential customers an accurate, nuanced picture of what to expect.
2.1.5 Location Privacy and Data Security
As mobile applications become more reliant on constant location data, user privacy issues have developed in tandem. Shaham et al. [12] investigated the significance of location privacy for users of location-based applications, discovering that many users are unaware of the extent to which their location data is gathered and potentially disclosed. Their study calls for greater transparency and user control in LBS applications—principles that CebSpot must embed in its data-handling practices.
Primault et al. [13] provided a detailed study of computational location privacy, documenting the history of privacy-preserving approaches and highlighting outstanding issues. Their work gives a technical path for developers who want to add strong privacy safeguards without sacrificing service quality. For CebSpot, this entails implementing anonymization and aggregation approaches that allow the platform to profit from user location data while limiting individual exposure.
Taken together, these investigations highlight CebSpot's duty to protect user data. The application's development must follow privacy-by-design principles, which require that data collection be minimal, intentional, and explicitly expressed to users via accessible privacy policies and in-app disclosures.
2.1.6 Mobile Application Adoption and Use in the Philippine Context
The Philippine environment provides a rich backdrop for analyzing mobile application adoption habits and problems. Gonzales et al. [14] used the Mobile App Rating Scale (MARS) to conduct a comprehensive search and quality assessment of mobile applications in the Philippines during the COVID-19 epidemic. Their findings emphasized the expanding role of mobile platforms in meeting everyday demands in the Philippines, as well as the importance of usability and design quality in determining application effectiveness.
According to DataReportal's Digital 2023 study on the Philippines [15], the country has one of Southeast Asia's highest rates of mobile internet usage, with a sizable segment of the population accessing the web mostly via smartphones. This data significantly supports CebSpot's viability as a mobile-first platform, given the prevalence of smartphone use among the target demographic in Cebu City.
Several studies have also investigated the adoption of specific mobile applications in the Philippine setting. Gumasing et al. [16] assessed the usability of transport network vehicle service applications, notably Grab, Angkas, and JoyRide, during the pandemic, highlighting usability factors that were important to user satisfaction. Prasetyo and Vallespin [17] used an expanded UTAUT framework to investigate factors influencing Grab Philippines' acceptability, focusing on the roles of performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and social impact in driving adoption.
Napalang and Regidor [18] investigated the rise of ride-hailing services in Metro Manila. At the same time, Semblante, Rabago, and Penuela [19] conducted a scoping analysis of food delivery service applications in densely populated Philippine cities. Both studies highlight the increasing integration of location-based mobile services into urban Filipinos' everyday routines, offering significant insights into user behavior patterns relevant to CebSpot's target demographic.
Maravilla and Gantalao [20] investigated e-tourism adoption among travel firms in Cebu City, concluding that digital platforms play an increasingly important role in enabling tourism-related transactions in the region. Their findings are directly applicable to CebSpot's aim of promoting local leisure areas and improving Cebu City's tourism landscape. Furthermore, Aguirre, Patricio, and Dela Cruz [21] investigated the adoption of the Waze mobile application among Generation Z in the Philippines, discovering that ease of use and perceived usefulness are the strongest predictors of continued application use, findings that inform CebSpot's user interface and experience design.
Finally, Zubiate, Calongos, and Andrade-Arenas [22] detailed the development of a mobile application for a metropolitan transportation system, focusing on the operational issues and user feedback loops required to sustain a mobile platform in an urban context. Their experience emphasizes the necessity of iterative development and community engagement to keep CebSpot responsive to its consumers' evolving demands in Cebu City.
2.2 Related Studies
2.2.1 Community-Driven Review Platforms and Consumer Decision-Making
Several studies have looked into how user-generated review systems affect customer behavior and trustworthiness. Research has found that review valence, reviewer profile, and platform familiarity all influence the perceived credibility of online reviews, with users interpreting profile cues in combination when assessing whether a reviewer can be trusted [23]. Furthermore, studies have demonstrated that online ratings carry measurable economic weight, with a one-star increase in a venue's rating correlated with a notable rise in revenues, particularly for independent establishments [24]. However, the integrity of review systems remains a persistent concern, as research has highlighted the dynamic and inconsistent nature of review filtering systems, noting that reviews can routinely shift between visible and filtered classifications, calling into question the reliability of platform-assigned labels as stable ground truth [25]. These findings are directly relevant to CebSpot, as they highlight both the power and the potential pitfalls of community-driven review ecosystems—informing the design of the platform's rating mechanisms and content moderation features.
2.2.2 Fake Review Detection and Platform Integrity in Location-Based Applications
As location-based platforms increasingly rely on user-generated content, the authenticity of that content has become a critical concern. A case study on fake review detection in a major mapping platform found that fabricated reviews are vulnerable to manipulation, with fake accounts predominantly awarding the maximum five-star rating, making detection more complex and raising concerns about the trustworthiness of aggregated scores [26].
The scale of the problem is significant, as research has documented that major platforms have had to block or remove tens of millions of policy-violating reviews and fake business profiles within a single year [26]. These findings underscore the importance of building strong community moderation tools and verification mechanisms into CebSpot from the ground up, particularly as the platform scales its venue listings across Cebu City and user-contributed content grows in volume.
2.2.3 Gamification and User Engagement in Location-Based Social Networks
Gamification has been extensively researched as a means of maintaining user engagement and improving platform statistics. Research examining the effectiveness of gamification mechanisms in location-based social networks found that user scores, an aggregate measure based on recent check-in activities, reflect a snapshot of users' temporal and spatial orders and behaviors, and that such social gaming elements help keep users actively engaged on the platform [29]. A related study found that location-based social media apps provide both hedonic and utilitarian benefits through gratification factors, including gamification, socialization, and self-purpose, with hedonic benefit fully mediating the relationship between these gratification factors and users' continued intention to use the platform [27]. Additionally, qualitative research has shown that gamification elements in location-based platforms can add a playful layer to physical spaces, drawing from concepts of hybrid space and spatial legibility to explain how game mechanics can shape users' real-world mobility decisions [28]. For CebSpot, these findings validate the potential of engagement features such as contribution milestones and activity streaks to sustain community participation and keep the platform's venue information current and reliable.
2.2.4 Electronic Word-of-Mouth, User Trust, and Satisfaction in Review Platforms
The role of electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) in shaping user trust and satisfaction on review sites has been extensively studied in the tourism and hospitality industries. Research on a major travel review platform discovered a direct and positive association between users' privacy views and their contentment with the platform, as well as a direct and positive relationship between eWOM and user trust [30]. Furthermore, an analysis of a large corpus of travel reviews revealed that the diversity and sentiment polarity of textual reviews are associated with higher overall customer satisfaction scores, implying that a balanced and varied review ecosystem contributes more meaningfully to user experience than uniformly positive feedback [31]. For CebSpot, these findings reinforce the importance of building a transparent and privacy-respecting review environment where diverse, genuine user experiences are surfaced—rather than curated or filtered toward uniformly favorable impressions.
2.2.5 Hyperlocal Venue Discovery and Mobile-First Platforms in Emerging Markets
Studies on venue discovery platforms operating in emerging market contexts offer particularly relevant insights for CebSpot. Research on hyperlocal food and venue discovery platforms has shown that a strong foundation in search, discovery, and user-generated content can serve as a springboard for broader service expansion, even in highly competitive markets [32]. Academic case analyses further highlight how the success of venue discovery platforms among young urban users is built on providing comprehensive information—including images, costs, menus, and community reviews—alongside forums where users can share firsthand experiences [32].
The experience of platforms that have operated in the Philippine market specifically also illustrates that while international venue discovery services have attempted to establish a presence in the country, many have eventually scaled back or exited local markets, leaving a gap for locally focused alternatives [32]. This gap is precisely what CebSpot aims to fill—offering a hyperlocal, community-driven discovery platform tailored to the specific social and geographic context of Cebu City.
2.3 Synthesis of the Reviewed Literature and Studies
The studied literature provides a solid theoretical and empirical foundation for CebSpot. The combination of location-based services, social networking elements, sophisticated recommendation systems, and community-driven evaluations creates a well-validated design paradigm for mobile discovery applications. Studies on location privacy and data security provide further context for the development team's ethical duties.
In the Philippines, widespread mobile internet penetration and the demonstrated success of location-based platforms like Grab, Waze, and food delivery apps demonstrate the local market's readiness for a venue-finding tool like CebSpot. The highlighted gaps in the literature, specifically the lack of a centralized, dependable, and locally oriented platform for locating hangout and leisure areas in Cebu City, confirm the suggested application's novelty and necessity.
By combining insights from these various yet interconnected bodies of knowledge, CebSpot is positioned to meet genuine, documented consumer demands in Cebu City while contributing to the growing ecosystem of mobile applications adapted to the Filipino context.
2.4 Research Gap
Despite the growing body of literature surrounding location-based services, point-of-interest recommendation systems, community-driven review platforms, and mobile application adoption in the Philippine context, several critical gaps remain that CebSpot directly seeks to address.
First, while studies on location-based social networks and venue discovery platforms are abundant globally, research focused specifically on hyperlocal hangout spot discovery in Philippine cities — particularly Cebu City — is largely absent. Most social information systems in developing countries are adopted from the developed world and often fail to account for local socio-geographical settings and cultural complexities, resulting in continued reliance on word-of-mouth [33]. Internationally developed platforms dominate the Philippine digital landscape yet fall short of addressing the hyperlocal discovery needs of local communities.
Second, the Philippine-specific literature reviewed remains heavily concentrated on ride-hailing services, food delivery applications, and transportation systems [16, 17, 18, 19, 22], with none addressing the domain of leisure and hangout spot discovery. Despite the country's high mobile internet usage [15], no study has examined how young urban Filipinos discover recreational venues through dedicated mobile platforms.
Third, while Maravilla and Gantalao [20] examined e-tourism adoption in Cebu City, their work focused on the supply side of tourism rather than community-driven discovery of local leisure venues. No study has been identified that evaluates how ordinary residents discover and evaluate hangout spots in Cebu City through digital means.
Fourth, the literature on review platform integrity [23, 26, 30, 31] has been conducted almost exclusively on large, globally operating platforms. The specific issues of sustaining review authenticity inside a small-scale, geographically bounded platform servicing a single city have received little attention, a gap that is especially pertinent to CebSpot as it grows its local reviewer community from a tiny user base.
Finally, and most importantly, no current dedicated platform offers a community-driven, mobile-first solution for finding hangout locations in Cebu City. Research has noted that hyperlocal information in developing countries is valued but often dispersed, constantly evolving, and poorly accessible through existing digital tools [33]. The continued reliance by many Cebu residents on social media influencers and unverified content for venue discovery underscores the absence of a trusted, locally focused alternative — a gap that CebSpot has set out to fill directly.
CHAPTER 3
METHODOLOGY
This chapter presents the methods, procedures, and development processes used in creating CebSpot: A Mobile Application for Discovery and Leisure in Cebu City. It discusses the research design, data-gathering procedures, system development methodology, system analysis, design, architecture, and development phases applied throughout the study. The methodology served as the framework that guided the researchers in ensuring that the application was systematically planned, designed, developed, and evaluated to meet the needs of its intended users.
The study adopted a developmental research approach supported by descriptive research methods to gather user insights and evaluate system requirements. Furthermore, the researchers utilized the Agile Scrum framework to support iterative software development, allowing continuous testing, refinement, and improvement of system features. Through this methodology, the study aimed to develop a reliable, user-centered, and technically functional mobile platform for discovering hangout spots in Cebu City.
3.1 Conceptual Framework
The study's conceptual framework employs the Input–Process–Output (IPO) model to illustrate the operational flow of CebSpot. The IPO model illustrates how the system gathers required inputs, processes the collected data, and produces outputs that support the application's core functionalities. Through this model, the interaction between user information, system operations, and generated results is clearly represented within the CebSpot platform.
Figure 1: IPO Model
3.1.1 Explanation of Conceptual Framework
Input: A phase captures the essential data that enters the system. This includes the user's location, determined by GPS coordinates or a manually entered address; search queries that include keywords, venue names, or categories; user preferences such as distance, price, and venue-type filters; and the venue database, which contains business information, operating hours, and contact information. These parameters define the user's search criteria and location.
Process: The phases indicate the internal mechanisms the system uses to handle and transform inputs into meaningful results. Data validation filters and verifies user inputs to ensure accuracy before processing. GPS services then calculate the distance and proximity to nearby venues. A database query compares the user's search parameters with the available venue data. Finally, trust verification confirms the legitimacy of user-submitted reviews, ensuring that the platform's content remains credible and reliable.
Output: phase delivers the final results to the user. These include a sorted list of venue results with ratings, an interactive map with a visual pin overlay, detailed venue information such as photos, reviews, and other relevant details, a booking confirmation with reservation details, and a route line for navigation in the area.
3.2 Research Design
This study used a developmental research design that centered on the methodical design, development, and evaluation of CebSpot: A Mobile Application for Discovery and Leisure in Cebu City. The Agile Software Development approach was used to guide the development process, enabling the research team to iteratively refine features and resolve issues across successive development cycles through continuous testing and user feedback.
To supplement the developmental approach, descriptive research methodologies were used. A survey was administered to target users in Cebu City to collect information on their venue discovery behaviors, preferences, and pain points with existing platforms. The findings had a direct impact on CebSpot's design and feature decisions throughout its development. These two approaches ensured that the program was both technically sound and truly responsive to the needs of its target users.
3.3 Data Gathering Procedures
The study utilized both primary and secondary data collection methods to support the development and evaluation of CebSpot.
Secondary Data Collection: Prior to development, a thorough analysis of relevant research and literature was carried out. On location-based services, point-of-interest recommendation systems, community-driven review platforms, and mobile application uptake in the Philippine context, scholarly journals, conference papers, and published reports were analyzed. The findings from this review directly informed the conceptual framework and design decisions of CebSpot.
Primary Data Collection: A structured survey questionnaire was distributed to target users in Cebu City to collect information on their current venue discovery behaviors, degree of satisfaction with existing platforms, preferred application features, and willingness to contribute reviews to a dedicated local platform. The survey was delivered online using social media sites and messaging programs popular among the target population.
Expert Evaluation: Selected technical experts and application evaluators provided feedback on CebSpot's functionality, usability, and design. Their feedback was used to improve the application before its final deployment.
Usability Testing: A usability testing phase was carried out with a subset of target users who interacted with the CebSpot application. The observations and feedback gathered during this phase were used to identify and remedy usability concerns, creating a seamless user experience.
3.3.1 Ethical Considerations
This study was conducted in accordance with ethical standards, ensuring that all participants' rights, privacy, and well-being were protected throughout the research process.
Informed Consent: Before participating, all respondents were told about the study's nature, goal, and scope. Participants were informed that their participation was fully voluntary and that they might withdraw at any time without consequence. Before conducting the survey and usability testing, all participants provided informed consent.
Confidentiality and Anonymity: All data collected from responders were kept strictly confidential. Personal information provided by participants was used strictly for this study and was not released to any third parties. Responses were anonymized so that individual participants could not be recognized in the research findings or published outputs.
Data Privacy: The study followed the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173) of the Philippines, which governs the collection, management, and protection of personal information. All collected data were securely stored and available only to members of the research team.
Respect for Participants: The research team made certain that all participants were treated with respect and dignity throughout the study. Throughout the data collection process, no participant was subjected to any type of pressure, manipulation, or harm.
Responsible Use of Data: All collected data were utilized solely for scholarly reasons. The study's conclusions were reported honestly and accurately, with no misrepresentation or falsification of data.

3.4.1.1 User Requirements Specification (URS)
The system is intended to be used by three main types of users:
Spotters (Primary Users): Spotters can take a photo of the spot through the app. Then they will provide the details, reviews, and route; you can reserve a spot if the place has one available.
Store Owners (Secondary Users): Owners can list and promote their spot, view their reviews, and view reservations directly if they register for the reservation system.
Admins (Managers): Admins manage user accounts, grant store owners access to the reservation system (if they register), and moderate user-submitted reports.
3.4.1.2 Functional Specification
User Authentication: The system allows users, store owners, and administrators to register and log in securely, with role-based access.
Spot Discovery: The system allows users to search, browse, and discover popular, nearby, trending, and hidden spots in Cebu City.
Map and Location Services: The system uses GPS and map features to show nearby venues, distance, navigation routes, and location-based recommendations.
Venue Management: The system allows store owners to create, update, and manage their venue listings, including photos, descriptions, operating hours, contact details, and reservation availability.
Review and Rating: The system allows users to rate venues, write reviews, and share experiences to help other users make better decisions.
Admin Management: The system allows administrators to manage users, approve venue listings, moderate reviews, and maintain platform content.
3.4.1.3 Non-Functional Requirements
Performance: The system should load venue listings, maps, search results, and reservation pages within a reasonable response time to provide a smooth user experience.
Usability: The application should have a simple, user-friendly interface that allows users, store owners, and administrators to navigate and use the system easily.
Security: The system should protect user accounts, passwords, reservation details, and payment-related information from unauthorized access.
Reliability: The system should be available and functional most of the time, especially when users are searching for venues, making reservations, or processing deposit payments.
Data Privacy: The system should protect users' personal information and location data by only collecting necessary data and requesting permission before accessing GPS services.
Compatibility: The application should run properly on supported Android devices and should work with required services such as GPS, maps, notifications, and payment integration.

Level 1 Diagram
The Level 1 DFD expands the CebSpot system into six internal processes, each representing a core functional area of the application. P1 (User Authentication) handles credential input from all user roles and interacts with the User Database (DS1) to verify and store account information. 3.6.5 Class Diagram
The class diagram in Figure 3.8 illustrates the object-oriented structure of the CebSpot system, defining the six core classes, their attributes and methods, and the relationships among them.
The User class represents all registered Spotters on the platform. It holds attributes for identification, authentication, and profile information such as user ID, name, email, password, role, profile photo, and location. It exposes methods for registration, login, profile management, and logout. The Spot class represents a venue or hangout location listed on CebSpot. It stores essential venue information, including the spot's name, category, description, address, GPS coordinates, photos, operating hours, and approval status, and provides methods for creating, updating, and deleting listings.
The review class captures user-generated feedback submitted for a spot. It includes a rating field, a comment body, a posting date, and a verification flag to support the platform's trust and authenticity mechanism and exposes methods for submitting, editing, and deleting reviews. The Reservation class handles table or slot bookings made by spotters at participating venues. It records the date, time, party size, reservation status, and any special notes submitted by the user and provides methods for creating, confirming, and canceling reservations.
The Admin class represents the platform manager role. It stores access-level credentials and exposes methods to approve or reject spot listings, moderate user-generated reviews, manage user accounts, view analytics, and generate platform reports. The Payment class handles deposit transactions associated with reservations. It stores the payment method, transaction amount, date, and status, and provides methods to process payments, issue refunds, and retrieve payment status.
In terms of relationships, a user writes zero or more reviews and makes zero or more reservations. A spot can accept zero or more reservations and have zero or more reviews associated with it. Each reservation is linked to exactly one payment record. The Admin class maintains a dependency relationship with the User class for account management purposes and with the Review class for content moderation, reflecting the administrator's oversight role across the platform.

3.7.2 Database Design
Figure 3.15 Database Design of Cebspot
The CebSpot database was designed and implemented using Supabase, an open-source backend platform built on PostgreSQL. The schema comprises eight interrelated tables, each representing a core entity of the system. Figure 3.17 illustrates the complete database structure and the relationships between tables.
The AUTH_USERS table serves as the system's authentication layer, storing each user's unique identifier, email address, encrypted password, and account creation timestamp. This table is managed directly by Supabase's built-in authentication service and acts as the root reference for all user-related data in the system.
The PROFILES table extends the authentication record by storing additional user information, including full name, email, avatar URL, role, and account creation date. It is linked to the AUTH_USERS table via a foreign key on user_id, ensuring that each profile corresponds to a verified, authenticated account.
The SPOTS table holds all venue listings on the platform. It stores the spot's unique identifier, owner reference, name, description, category, address, GPS coordinates (latitude and longitude), image URL, opening hours, reservability flag, approval status, and creation timestamp. Each spot is linked to its owner through the owner_id foreign key referencing the PROFILES table.
The OWNER_REQUESTS table manages Store Owner registration requests submitted to the platform. It captures the applicant's user reference, business name, business permit URL, contact number, current request status, and the administrator who reviewed the request. This table facilitates the verification workflow before a user is granted Store Owner privileges.
The REVIEWS table records all user-generated ratings and feedback for a venue. It stores the reviewer's user reference, the spot reference, a numerical rating, a written comment, an optional image URL, and the date the review was posted. It is linked to both the PROFILES and SPOTS tables through foreign keys.
The SPOT_TABLES table defines the physical tables or slots available for reservation at a given venue. Each record stores a reference to its parent spot, a table name, seating capacity, current availability status, and creation timestamp. This table enables fine-grained reservation management at the table level.
The RESERVATIONS table manages all booking transactions made by Spotters. It references the user, spot, and specific table being reserved and stores the reservation date and time, group size, special requests, reservation status, payment status, total amount, and creation timestamp. This table serves as the central transaction record linking Spotters, venues, and payments together.
The PAYMENTS table handles all deposit transactions associated with reservations. It stores the linked reservation reference, user reference, payment amount, payment method, payment status, checkout session identifier, payment transaction identifier, payment confirmation timestamp, and record creation timestamp. Payment records are created upon reservation submission and updated once the venue owner confirms receipt.
The ACTIVITIES table logs all significant user actions performed within the platform. It records the user reference, the type of action performed, the target entity type, the target entity identifier, and the timestamp of the activity. This table supports the platform's audit trail and feeds into the administrator's analytics dashboard.
3.8 System Architecture
3.8.1 Architectural Design
The system architecture of CebSpot follows a client-server model organized into four distinct layers, as illustrated in Figure 3.16. The presentation layer serves as the frontend of the system, developed using React Native, and provides the user interface through which spotters, store owners, and administrators interact with the application. All user inputs and actions originate from this layer and are transmitted to the server for processing. The application layer contains the core business logic of CebSpot, responsible for processing all requests received from the mobile application, including user authentication, search queries, location requests, spot recommendations, map-based discovery, reservation processing, review management, and administrator controls, before returning the appropriate responses to the presentation layer. The Service Integration Layer connects the system to external third-party services necessary for full functionality, including GPS and map services for location data and navigation routes, payment gateways for secure reservation deposit processing, and notification services for sending booking confirmations and system alerts to users in real time. Finally, the data layer serves as the backend database of the system built on Supabase and PostgreSQL, storing and managing all platform data, including user accounts, venue listings, reservation records, reviews, ratings, favorites, payment records, and administrator-managed data, ensuring that all information is securely stored and accurately retrieved whenever needed by the application layer.

Row-level security policies were configured to restrict data access based on each user’s authenticated role, ensuring data privacy and security across all user types.
